# Weekly Teaching Guide — Prototype E

> Adopted instructor/Teaching Assistant working format. Use one weekly overview page, then one separate full page for each instructional day. A normal Monday-Friday guide is six pages. Use week/day identifiers without calendar dates. Follow the learning sequence, adjust to the available block, and record where each section stops. Student-facing materials require instructor review before use.

## Print Setup

US Letter portrait; 0.5-inch margins; Arial; black text; blue headings (`#366091`). Use 20 pt bold centered titles, 13 pt bold section headings, 12 pt body/subtitle, and 11 pt supporting notes. Single line spacing; balance paragraph/section spacing to fill the printable area. Increase spacing or type on shorter pages as useful. Use an explicit page break before each daily title. Keep headings with their content; retain usable note-writing space. Render and inspect every page.

Dense pages require concise wording and references to governing recipes/SOPs, while preserving essential learning, safety, and authorization. Do not shrink body text to force a fit.

See the [standard](../09_Workflows/Daily_Teaching_Guide_Standard.md) and [design guidance](../09_Workflows/Document_Design_Standard.md).

### Before Class — Set Up

Materials, instructor preparation, and required verification/authorization.

### Teach / Show

**Recipe-to-action check:** Select one or two meaningful directions from the actual recipe. Ask students what the verb means, what action it requires, and what visible, audible, aromatic, tactile or measured cue tells them it is working or complete. Teach unfamiliar or safety-critical steps before work. Keep this brief and oral/practical; do not add a routine worksheet.

Teach only what is new, unsafe, misunderstood, or needed for the work. Embed likely errors and essential safety, quality, and understanding checks here.

### Students Do

**Core Learning:** essential work and individual evidence, including opening, immediate plan, and safety/authorization gates. Make individual evidence observable: can the student explain the instruction, carry it out safely, notice a result, and adjust or request help appropriately? Distinguish coached execution from independent transfer; do not infer knowledge from a finished group dish.

**Flexible Learning / adjustment:** useful continuation when ready, what may move if time is lost, and the clean stopping point. Keep this within Students Do.

### Micro-Skills / If They Come Up

Expected just-in-time micro-skills and useful refinements. Keep these compact rather than making every micro-skill a separate objective, worksheet, or grade.

### Clean Stop

- **Finish:** logical stop point; secure/store/label, handoff, clean/reset, and release as required by current procedures.
- **Exit:** concise student-language reflection or evidence prompt (for example, "What did the recipe ask you to do, and what told you it worked?"). Keep it short; no automatic written response.

### Supporting Note — Only When Useful

Predictable student question/confirmed answer, essential question, instructor context, or day-specific adult roles that differ from the overview.
