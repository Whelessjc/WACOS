# Bistro Prep and Service Rhythms

## Purpose

Bistro is the authentic performance environment for the fundamentals students build from the first day of Culinary. The food matters, but the larger test is whether students can work safely, professionally, and usefully inside an operation.

Instructor teaching language:

**I don't need to know if you can cook. I need to know if you can work.**

This does not mean cooking skill is unimportant. It means Bistro performance depends on the complete operating system around the food: readiness, planning, communication, awareness, execution, sanitation, service, handoff, and reset.

## Weekly Arc

**Monday-Wednesday — BUILD THE FUNDAMENTALS**

Instruction, practice, correction, food and workplace safety, recipe literacy, technique, communication, and Professional Thinking build the habits Bistro will test.

**Thursday — PLAN THE WORK**

Use the Bistro Prep Rhythm.

**Friday — WORK THE PLAN**

Use the Bistro Rhythm.

The phrases **Plan the Work** and **Work the Plan** are used as operating language without a named attribution unless reliable sourcing is established.

## Work Ready and Kitchen Ready

**Work Ready** is the broad program readiness expectation. Students arrive on time and ready to engage in Culinary work. The current Bistro arrival routine includes:

**ID → Clock In → Lock In → Park It → Stow Belongings → Notebook → Pen → A Plan → Report to Station**

**Kitchen Ready** remains the specific safety/access gate for practical kitchen participation. Current Kitchen Readiness requirements are governed separately and include proper footwear. Work Ready does not replace Kitchen Ready.

## Graceful Urgency

Bistro may already have guests present when students arrive. Students transition from the school day into the operation with **graceful urgency**: purposeful movement, calm communication, and immediate action without running, panic, or chaos.

The operation does not wait for students to socialize, wander, or be individually launched by an adult. Students move through the Work Ready routine and begin opening their assigned station.

## Bistro Prep Rhythm — Thursday: Plan the Work

Thursday Bistro Prep is not a normal team lab. It is production work inside one larger operation.

Students normally receive individual production assignments. Pairs may be used when the work reasonably benefits from two people. Students retain their Monday-Thursday cleaning-job responsibilities while owning their assigned production.

### 1. Arrive On Time and Work Ready

Complete the current Work Ready routine and transition into production with graceful urgency.

### 2. Huddle

Begin with a brief whole-group Bistro Huddle. Cover only what is different or especially important for the upcoming service, such as:

- menu or specials;
- special guests or reservations when relevant;
- expected volume;
- schedule or timing changes;
- shortages or 86'd items;
- unusual production/service conditions;
- one important carryover from the previous service.

Permanent station expectations remain on checklists. The huddle is not another lecture.

### 3. Receive the Production Assignment

Know exactly what product or task you own and whether the assignment is individual or paired.

### 4. Set Up the Weekly Cleaning Job

Dishes, Counters & Surfaces, Storage, Floors, and Laundry responsibilities continue on Thursday. Individual production does not erase the student's weekly operating responsibility.

### 5. Read Before Acting

Read the complete recipe or production direction before touching ingredients. Identify product, yield, assigned quantity, units, equipment, sequence, and stopping/storage needs.

### 6. Set the Station and Mise en Place

Organize the station and gather the complete mise en place before production begins. A calm, organized station supports safe and efficient work.

### 7. Production Preflight — No Checkoff, No Production

Before production, the student must be able to show or state:

- What am I making?
- What is the recipe yield?
- How much am I assigned to make?
- Am I making the recipe as written, or has scaling been assigned?
- What units am I using?
- Have I read the complete procedure?
- Is my mise en place complete?
- What happens first?

A notebook entry plus a brief instructor/Teaching Assistant verification is sufficient unless another current form is required. Do not create unnecessary paperwork.

### 8. Produce and Clean as You Go

Follow the recipe, current safety requirements, and product standard. Maintain the station and contribute to the weekly cleaning job while working.

### 9. Clean Stop

Normally stop production at least 15 minutes before the bell. Do not turn unfinished production into frantic production. Identify a logical and safe stopping point.

### 10. Store, Handoff, and Reset

When work carries into another class or student, follow current storage and labeling procedures and leave useful handoff notes identifying:

- what the product/work is;
- what has been completed;
- what still needs to happen;
- where the product/components are located.

The next person should be able to continue without reconstructing what happened. Clean and reset the station before leaving.

## Bistro Rhythm — Friday: Work the Plan

### 1. Arrive On Time and Work Ready

Have what the work requires. Complete the current Work Ready routine and report to the assigned Bistro station with graceful urgency.

### 2. Open Your Station

Use the current station checklist. Get ready before the work arrives.

### 3. Own Your Area

Know what your team is responsible for and take care of it. Do not assume another team or an adult will finish your responsibilities.

### 4. Communicate

Listen. Speak clearly. Use callbacks and current service language. Share information other people need. Do not make the operation guess.

### 5. Stay Aware — See the Whole Operation

Life of an Order is not only a diagram. Recognize it happening around you: guests arriving, queue forming, orders entering, tickets building, production underway, food/drinks ready, guests eating, beverages getting low, plates finishing, tables clearing, dishes building, and reset needs appearing.

Every team asks:

**What do you notice?**

**What does it mean?**

**What needs to happen next?**

Awareness does not mean abandoning your assigned role. Respond within your training, authorization, and responsibilities.

### 6. Be Useful

There is always useful work in the operation. Notice what needs to happen next, help within your role, restock, communicate, correct what you can, and do not wait for an adult to manufacture every next task.

### 7. Do the Work

Follow the ticket, recipe, build reference, checklist, procedure, or direction. Execute the fundamentals under pressure.

### 8. Clean as You Go

Maintain the station while working. Do not save the entire mess for the end.

### 9. Reset Your Station

Leave the station completely ready for the next class, shift, or service. Earlier classes may reset rather than perform final shutdown.

### 10. Close When Required

When the class is responsible for final shutdown, complete the additional station and whole-operation closing responsibilities using current checklists and safety/storage procedures.

## Clean as You Go vs. Reset vs. Close

These are different responsibilities:

- **Clean as You Go:** maintain safe, organized work while operating.
- **Reset:** leave the station ready for whoever comes next.
- **Close:** perform final shutdown when there is no next service/class requiring the station.

Students are not done because the food is finished.

## Required Pre-Opening Training Outcomes

Before the first public Bistro service, both Culinary 1 and Culinary 2 should experience the current operating system. Culinary 2 recalibrates to current WACOS procedures regardless of where or when prior Culinary experience occurred; prior experience does not substitute for current-system training. The instructor may accelerate when students demonstrate current competence.

Required outcomes include:

- Work Ready and Kitchen Ready routines;
- Life of an Order and all five current teams;
- graceful-urgency arrival/opening rehearsal;
- whole-group Service simulation in a populated dining room;
- Barista operating-system practice;
- situational awareness across all five teams;
- Practice Bistro Prep using low-stakes/non-menu production work;
- Production Preflight;
- clean-stop and handoff-note practice;
- whole-operation Reset & Closing rehearsal;
- cumulative food-safety and workplace-safety application;
- at least one complete mock-service cycle, which may be brief and use simulated or simplified food.

Week 4 is governed by these required outcomes rather than a rigid Monday-Friday sequence. The instructor may rearrange, combine, extend, or accelerate training based on calendar, section progress, and readiness.

## Practice Bistro Prep

Practice Bistro Prep is a required pre-opening training experience. Use low-stakes production tasks rather than the actual Bistro prep list so the purpose remains learning the production-management system rather than gaining an extra day of real prep.

Rehearse:

**Work Ready → Huddle → Assignment → Weekly Job Setup → Read → Mise en Place → Production Preflight → Produce → Clean Stop → Handoff/Store → Reset**

## Whole-Group Service Training

Initial Service training should be whole-group and instructor-led so students see a populated dining room and the complete guest experience. The Teaching Assistant reinforces and coaches the established system afterward.

Students practice recognizing and responding to guests arriving, queue-number flow, orders entering, food/drinks becoming ready, guest needs, refills, clearing, and table reset. Service circulates with purpose rather than hovering around the stand.

## Barista Training

Students do not need to memorize every drink before service. They need to know how to operate Barista:

**Open → Organize → Receive Ticket → Use Build Reference → Produce → Verify → Handoff → Complete Ticket → Clean/Restock → Reset/Close**

Current drink-build references remain available at the station.

## Cumulative Safety Performance

Bistro Prep and Service are cumulative food-safety and workplace-safety performance environments.

In Culinary 1, expectations accumulate as practices are introduced and practiced. In Culinary 2, students recalibrate to current WACOS systems and then move toward **safety fluency under pressure**.

Once a practice has been taught, students are expected to apply it during authentic work without the instructor having to announce that it is the safety target for that day.

## Launch Target

Week 5 is the normal first-public-service target for both fall and spring Culinary 1 cohorts and the aligned opening/relaunch target for the operating program when the calendar allows. The instructor retains authority to delay, reduce, modify, or pause Bistro service for readiness, safety, or calendar conditions.

Bistro is not a separate unit students suddenly encounter in Week 4. Weeks 1-3 deliberately build the component fundamentals; Week 4 assembles those fundamentals into Prep and Service systems; Week 5 begins authentic performance.
