# Lesson Sequence: Customer Service and Bistro Readiness

Rewritten 2026-07 using the real dining room operations training materials (Customer_Service.pptx) instead of an abstract version. Source note: E.P.I.C. values referenced in older training materials are retired and are not used here (see DECISION_REGISTER.md D-note on E.P.I.C.).

## Day 1: Life of an Order and Service vs. Hospitality
- Opening routine: entry, agenda check, huddle.
- Demo or mini-lesson: Life of an Order - guest arrival to farewell. Teach the distinction explicitly: "Service is what you do to someone. Hospitality is how you make someone feel." Introduce the five teams: Dining owns guest experience; Barista owns beverage and pastry service; Kitchen owns food production; Support owns stewarding and operational cleanliness; Tournant / Expo owns flow with Expo as home base.
- Student work: trace an order through greeting, ticket, cooking, expo, running, clearing, dish, reset.
- Lab/application: role-play the current service flow: host greets first, completes OnTime/ILT check-in, assigns a number only to an ordering guest, and directs the guest to POS; POS calls the number, greets, takes, confirms, and enters the order.
- Cleanup/reset: guest area, service area, and dish path reset after role-play.
- Exit check: name one place an order can break down, and explain the difference between service and hospitality in your own words.

## Day 2: Beverages, Plates, Utensils, and Table Setting
- Opening routine: huddle on station ownership.
- Demo or mini-lesson: beverage service (filtered water, sweet/unsweetened tea, hot tea, how to make coffee, ice and scoops, pitchers). Types of plates (bread & butter, salad/appetizer, entree, specialty, bowls). Utensils (salad fork, dinner fork, soup spoon, spoon, butter knife, steak knife). Table setting standard: solids from the left, liquids from the right; napkin in lap; knife pointing toward the plate; drinks refilled from the right.
- Student work: practice filling beverage orders; practice a full table setting from the standard.
- Lab/application: station setup and reset practice using the real dining room layout (host stand, tables 11-14, 21-24, 31-33).
- Cleanup/reset: station inspection for tools, towels, trash, counters, and traffic flow.
- Exit check: what goes on the left, and what goes on the right?

## Day 3: Greeting, Check-In, POS, and Dining Awareness
- Opening routine: agenda and communication practice.
- Demo or mini-lesson: the current steps of service—host says “Good morning” before making any judgment, asks about Bistro ILT, uses OnTime, distinguishes dining from hanging out, assigns a queue number only to an ordering guest, and directs the order to Square POS. POS calls the number, makes eye contact, smiles, greets, takes and confirms the order, and enters it. Dining anticipates needs after Square entry rather than taking seated-table orders.
- Student work: practice the host/check-in and POS flows in pairs, including approved entry, denied entry, dining, and non-ordering guests.
- Lab/application: mock arrival, check-in, queue, POS, and Dining-awareness rounds using the real room layout.
- Cleanup/reset: dish, counters, service area, guest area, and floor reset.
- Exit check: what does the beverage napkin/coaster signal to the rest of the team?

## Day 4: Serving, Clearing, and Order Shorthand
- Opening routine: prep huddle.
- Demo or mini-lesson: serving standard - Left, Lower, Leave (approach from the guest's left, gently lower the plate, announce the dish and step back). Avoid reaching across a guest. Plate-clearing technique: fingers under the rim, keep plates level, limit to two plates at a time, clear from the right. Order shorthand: T (sweet tea), C (coffee), UT (unsweetened tea), W (water), Bod (bodega), Burro (burrito), Grit (grits bowl), Q (quiche), SOS (sauce on the side). - Student work: practice Left-Lower-Leave serving with a partner; practice writing real orders using shorthand.
- Lab/application: timed serving practice against the real benchmarks.
- Cleanup/reset: label, store, clean, reset.
- Exit check: what does "Left, Lower, Leave" mean, and why clear from the right?

## Day 5: Early-Release Full Mock Service
- Opening routine: readiness check, huddle, roles confirmed.
- Demo or mini-lesson: final service reminders—LATTE for guest recovery (Listen, Acknowledge, Take action, Thank, Ensure satisfaction), no arguing over questionable queue numbers, and dietary restrictions flagged immediately rather than guessed at.
- Student work: assigned prep and service setup using the appropriate side-work checklist (Dining, Barista, Kitchen, Support, or Tournant / Expo).
- Lab/application: shortened full mock service with no public guests. Practice ticket flow, expo, running, clearing, dish, and reset without treating the day as public service.
- Cleanup/reset: full service reset.
- Exit check: debrief one success and one system to improve before the first normal public service on Friday, September 11.


## Current Five-Team Training and Rotation

Use **Dining, Barista, Kitchen, Support, and Tournant / Expo**. The same instructional teams carry into Bistro and remain in each position for two consecutive services. Service 1 develops familiarity; Service 2 develops independence. Culinary 1 and Culinary 2 share one repeating ten-service cycle.

Life of an Order remains the foundation. Train Life of an Order; Dining + Barista; Kitchen + Support; Tournant / Expo + mock service; additional mock/check/reflection; targeted final training; real Bistro Prep; and Bistro Service. Week 4 remains training and mock service only, with no public guests or soft opening. Thursday prep retains weekly lab jobs; Friday service follows Bistro-position duties.

Tournant / Expo owns operational flow, with Expo as its home base. During active service one Tournant student remains responsible for Expo/pass while other trained and authorized members deploy. Use **Expo → Kitchen → Dining → Barista → Support → Expo** and ask: **What do you notice? What does it mean? What needs to happen next?**
