# Culinary 1 Curriculum

## Purpose
Define the one-semester Culinary 1 course as the foundational and exploratory entry point into West Ashley Culinary.

## Course Identity
Culinary 1 is a double-pathway, one-semester foundation and exploration course. It should give every student a durable skill set and a ServSafe Food Handler credential target whether or not the student continues in the culinary pathway.

The message to students is simple: if they love it, they can request Culinary 2 through their IGP. If they do not, the skill set still belongs to them forever.

## Design Principles
- Foundations before expensive product.
- Cook what you cut so knife practice becomes food, not waste.
- The Bistro is not a separate unit. It is the authentic performance environment where students demonstrate fundamentals taught from Day 1.
- Weeks 1-3 deliberately build the component habits Bistro will test; Week 4 assembles them into Prep and Service systems; Week 5 is the normal first-public-service target when readiness, safety, and the calendar allow.
- Culinary 1 should prepare many students for life and some students for Culinary 2.
- A semester course needs task-based targets, not abstract units.
- Recipes are vehicles. Students repeatedly practice reading, planning, mise en place, safe execution, communication, clean-as-you-go, clean stop, handoff, and reset.

## Weekly Bistro Arc

Once Bistro is operating, use:

- **Monday-Wednesday — BUILD THE FUNDAMENTALS**
- **Thursday — PLAN THE WORK** using the Bistro Prep Rhythm
- **Friday — WORK THE PLAN** using the Bistro Rhythm

See `../../02_Bistro/Operations/Bistro_Rhythms.md` for the current operating language.

## Work Ready and Kitchen Ready

**Work Ready** is the broad program readiness expectation. Students arrive on time and ready to engage in Culinary work. Current Bistro language includes ID, Clock In, Lock In, Park It, Notebook, Pen, and a Plan, followed by reporting to the assigned work area.

**Kitchen Ready** remains the specific safety/access gate for practical kitchen participation. It does not disappear inside the broader Work Ready concept.

## Pre-Bistro Fundamentals
Students should not be treated as Bistro-ready until they have been taught and have practiced the current foundations appropriate to their work:

- applied Food Handler safety and sanitation;
- handwashing, hygiene, Kitchen Readiness, and Work Ready routines;
- three-compartment sink and dish-machine systems;
- clean and sanitize procedures;
- laundry and towel systems;
- storage, labeling, dating, FIFO, and cooler awareness;
- recipe reading and a Production Preflight habit;
- mise en place before production;
- kitchen math foundations: fractions, units, conversions, yield, and scaling in authentic recipe work;
- basic equipment identification, retrieval, return, and safe use;
- foundational communication: behind, corner, hot, sharp, heard/callbacks, 86, and other current language as introduced;
- situational awareness: **What do you notice? What does it mean? What needs to happen next?**;
- clean-as-you-go discipline;
- clean-stop thinking and appropriate storage/handoff/reset;
- the five current Bistro teams: Service, Barista, Line Cooks, Support, and Expo / Floaters;
- Life of an Order;
- graceful-urgency arrival/opening practice;
- whole-group Service simulation in a populated dining room;
- Barista operating-system practice;
- Practice Bistro Prep using low-stakes/non-menu production assignments;
- whole-operation Reset & Closing rehearsal;
- at least one complete mock-service cycle, which may be brief and use simulated or simplified food.

Week 4 is governed by these required outcomes rather than a rigid day-by-day sequence. The instructor may rearrange, combine, extend, or accelerate training based on section progress, calendar, and readiness.

## Recipe Preflight and Clean Stop Begin Early

Do not wait until Bistro Prep to teach production-management habits.

From the earliest labs, progressively teach students to:

**READ → IDENTIFY PRODUCT/YIELD → CONFIRM QUANTITY/UNITS → PLAN → MISE EN PLACE → CHECK → BEGIN**

Early labs may require heavy instructor guidance. Independence increases through Weeks 1-3. By Bistro Prep, the Production Preflight is required before production begins.

Likewise, teach early that production does not continue recklessly until the bell. Normally stop cooking at least 15 minutes before the bell. Students learn to identify a logical clean stop, follow applicable storage/labeling procedures, communicate unfinished work when needed, clean, and reset.

## Thursday Bistro Prep

Thursday Bistro Prep is production work, not a normal team lab.

- Begin with a short whole-group Bistro Huddle covering only current-service differences such as specials, guests/reservations, volume, schedule changes, shortages/86s, unusual conditions, and important carryover learning.
- Students normally receive individual production assignments. Use pairs when the work reasonably benefits from two people.
- Monday-Thursday cleaning jobs remain active during Prep.
- Before production, students complete the current Production Preflight. Notebook plus brief adult verification is sufficient unless another current form is required. **No checkoff → no production.**
- Students produce and clean as they go.
- At clean stop, unfinished work is safely stored/labeled under current procedures and receives useful handoff notes before the station is reset.

Practice Bistro Prep occurs before the first real Bistro Prep and uses low-stakes/non-menu work so it teaches the system rather than becoming an extra production day.

## Friday Bistro Service

Use the permanent Bistro Rhythm:

1. Arrive On Time and Work Ready.
2. Open Your Station.
3. Own Your Area.
4. Communicate.
5. Stay Aware — See the Whole Operation.
6. Be Useful.
7. Do the Work.
8. Clean as You Go.
9. Reset Your Station.
10. Close When Required.

Instructor teaching language may use:

**I don't need to know if you can cook. I need to know if you can work.**

This emphasizes the complete operation around the food; it does not remove cooking skill from the curriculum.

## Suggested Semester Sequence
| Phase | Weeks | Focus | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Orientation and operating foundations | 1 | Room knowledge, Work Ready/Kitchen Ready, communication, recipe preflight, first production, reset | Students move safely, read before acting, and reset with support |
| Applied safety and stewardship | 2 | Hygiene, sanitation, dish systems, storage, temperature control, cleaning jobs, product stewardship | Procedural checks in authentic work |
| Recipe literacy and production organization | 3 | Equipment literacy, measuring, yield/units/scaling, mise en place, prep lists, clean stop/handoff | Marked recipe, preflight, mise/check, product carried through storage/reset |
| Assemble Bistro systems | 4 | Life of an Order, five teams, Service, Barista, graceful urgency, situational awareness, Practice Prep, Reset/Closing, mock service | Required pre-opening outcomes demonstrated with instructor coaching |
| Bistro launch | 5 | Targeted correction, real Prep, first public service | Live service evidence and complete reset |
| Knife and cooking progression | 6-10 | Safe cuts, onion/garlic, carrots, potatoes, saute, roast, steam, boil, stir fry, simple seasoning | Cook-what-you-cut labs, practical checks |
| Applied labs and technique development | 10-17 | Heat, flavor, proteins, frying, sauces, vegetables, eggs, pasta and other approved foundations | Lab/practical evidence plus weekly Bistro application |
| Bridge to Culinary 2 | 18 | Skill inventory, independent prep planning, final practical, reflection | Final practical, self-assessment, readiness conversation |

## Core Competencies
- Safety and sanitation.
- Work Ready and Kitchen Ready habits.
- Station setup, clean-as-you-go, reset, and close when required.
- Recipe reading and Production Preflight.
- Measuring, units, yield, and scaling.
- Mise en place and workflow.
- Basic knife handling.
- Basic cooking methods.
- Dish and cleaning systems.
- Guest service basics.
- Life of an Order and situational awareness.
- Professional communication.
- Personal responsibility and usefulness.

## Assessment Anchors
- ServSafe Food Handler credential target.
- Pre-Bistro fundamentals/readiness evidence.
- Bistro role performance after launch.
- Skills checklist or portfolio evidence when used.
- Lab execution and cleanup.
- Reflection/self-grade after service when assigned.

Current grade weights and assessment rules are governed by `../../03_Assessment/06_Assessment_System.md` and the Core Manual.
