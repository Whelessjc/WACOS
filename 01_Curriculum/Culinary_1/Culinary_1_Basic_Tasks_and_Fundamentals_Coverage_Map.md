# Culinary 1 — Basic Tasks & Fundamentals Coverage Map

## Purpose

Track the procedural fundamentals Culinary 1 deliberately teaches before and through Bistro. The Bistro is the authentic performance environment for these fundamentals; it is not a separate unit that begins after the fundamentals.

Weeks 1-3 build component habits. Week 4 assembles them into current Prep/Service systems. Week 5 is the normal first-public-service target when readiness, safety, and calendar allow.

Do not create a parallel curriculum or unnecessary sign-off bureaucracy. Use authentic demonstrations, instructor checkpoints, Daily Teaching Guides, current station references, and concise evidence where useful.

## Core Procedural Coverage

| Navigation / equipment | Scavenger hunt, room knowledge, named-item retrieval/return, equipment families | Students find what work requires without adult rescue |

## Recipe / Production Preflight Progression

Begin with the earliest labs and increase independence:

**READ → IDENTIFY PRODUCT/YIELD → CONFIRM QUANTITY/UNITS → PLAN → MISE EN PLACE → CHECK → BEGIN**

By Bistro Prep, students must identify product, yield, assigned quantity, scaling status, units, complete procedure, mise en place, and first step before production. Notebook + brief adult verification is sufficient unless another current form is required.

## Clean Stop and Handoff Progression

Students learn early that production does not continue recklessly until the bell. Normally stop cooking/production at least 15 minutes before dismissal.

When work is unfinished:

1. Stop at a logical point.
2. Follow current applicable storage/labeling procedure.
3. Communicate what is complete and what happens next.
4. Clean and reset.

During Bistro Prep, handoff notes identify what the work/product is, what is complete, what happens next, and where components are located.

## Week 4 Required Integration Outcomes

Before public service, students experience:

- Life of an Order and all five current teams;
- graceful-urgency arrival/opening;
- whole-group instructor-led Service simulation in a populated dining room;
- Barista operating-system practice;
- situational awareness across all teams;
- Practice Bistro Prep using low-stakes/non-menu production;
- Production Preflight;
- clean-stop/handoff practice;
- whole-operation Reset & Closing rehearsal;
- cumulative food/workplace safety;
- at least one complete mock-service cycle, which may be brief/simplified.

Week 4 is governed by these outcomes rather than a rigid day-by-day sequence.

## Bistro Performance Destination

Thursday: **PLAN THE WORK** using the Bistro Prep Rhythm.

Friday: **WORK THE PLAN** using the Bistro Rhythm.

Instructor teaching language may use:

**I don't need to know if you can cook. I need to know if you can work.**

The point is not to minimize cooking. It is to make clear that successful Culinary performance requires readiness, planning, safety, execution, communication, awareness, cleaning, handoff, and reset around the food.
