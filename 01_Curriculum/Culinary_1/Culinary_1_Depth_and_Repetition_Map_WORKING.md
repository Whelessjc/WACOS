# Culinary 1 — Depth and Repetition Map

**Status:** Working audit reference for instructor review; not a new course requirement or evidence of student mastery.
**Prepared:** October 12, 2026
**Scope:** Culinary 1, 18-week fall/spring course. Current WACOS modular pacing and detailed lesson sequences govern; actual section progress and accepted weekly DTGs override projected calendar pacing.

## Purpose

Map *transferable capabilities* to existing instructional opportunities and authentic Bistro work. Prevent "covered in a lesson" from being mistaken for demonstrated proficiency. Preserve depth over breadth: repeat useful skills through different recipes instead of adding techniques for novelty.

**Operating principle:** Teach before first needed use → demonstrate and practice → check actual performance → repeat in production → increase independence when evidence supports it.

## Evidence language (not a new grade or form)

- **Introduced:** explained, shown, or first attempted; does not establish competence.
- **Practiced with coaching:** student performs with reminders or correction.
- **Independent in a familiar task:** student performs the expected procedure safely and accurately without repeated prompting.
- **Transfer demonstrated:** student adapts the capability to another product or situation and explains the adjustment.
- **Not verified:** the plans identify an opportunity but actual student performance evidence is not available.

Safety and authorization gates apply at every stage. Students do not operate restricted equipment merely because they can explain a procedure. Record meaningful evidence in the existing DTG notes, lab observation, Bistro assessment, or manager log; **no new student tracking form**.

## Planning guardrails

1. **Do not equate frequency of mention with practice.** A weekly guide mentioning "reset" does not prove students did it correctly.
2. **Do not equate a finished recipe with proficiency.** The teacher needs an observable process cue, decision, or corrected result.
3. **Do not add labs solely to check off methods.** Favor an existing recipe that practices knife work, heat, timing, sanitation and closing together.
4. **Distinguish the reason for a gap:** never explicitly taught; taught but no individual check; checked with coaching; or performed inconsistently under service pressure.
5. **Safety-critical procedures are non-negotiable** even if rare (e.g., bloodborne-pathogen reporting: stop, isolate, notify adult, follow approved protocol).
6. **Use the current DTG as the live record.** Capture at most one or two high-value observations and the next corrective action rather than maintaining a parallel student checklist.
7. **Week labels are planning anchors, not claims about delivery.** The October 12–16, 2026 Week 10 fundamentals-recovery DTGs supersede the generic Week 10 heat-control suggestion for that specific week.

## Instructor evidence check (weekly, existing notes only)

- Which capability was the **primary target** this week?
- What did students **actually do**, and how much adult prompting was needed?
- What was **repeated in a different context**, if anything?
- What safety or operating error **must be retaught before next use**?
- Which next lab/Bistro assignment provides the **smallest authentic repetition**?

Do not mark a capability "mastered" merely because a recipe was completed or a checklist was printed.

## Culinary 1 → Culinary 2 handoff

Culinary 1 destination: safe, useful participation with growing consistency on core procedures and controlled techniques. Culinary 2 destination: more independent planning, quality control, adjustment, timing, anticipation and whole-operation responsibility. Prior Culinary 1 completion is **not** proof of current competence; Culinary 2 checks baseline and coaches gaps.
