# Culinary 1 - Week 1 Teaching Guide

> Canonical live guide for August 11-14, 2026. The older day-labeled DTGs remain supporting source records; daily labels below are suggested starting points, not required stopping boundaries.

## Weekly Destination

- **Course:** Culinary 1
- **Unit:** Orientation and Systems
- **Weekly Anchor:** Students can enter Kitchen Mode, locate essential kitchen areas and tools, read before acting, work with a team, and help reset a station.
- **Learning sequence:** Welcome and kitchen exploration -> Kitchen Mode and live routines -> read and mark the pancake recipe -> prepare mise en place -> pancake production -> full reset and brief reflection.
- **Knowledge:** Essential locations, routines, recipe parts, measurements, heat/doneness cues, and reset expectations.
- **Skill:** Locating, communicating, reading, measuring, preparing mise en place, introductory heat use, teamwork, and reset.
- **Professional thinking:** Preparation, awareness, precision, communication, stewardship, and reflection.

## Show / Students Do

| Sequence | Instructor shows or explains | Students do |
|---|---|---|
| Re-entry and room | High School, High Standards; Kitchen Mode; access and movement boundaries | Explore essential kitchen locations, use correct names, and return items |
| Live routines | Entry, handwashing, callouts, movement, and what reset means | Rehearse movement and communication; reset one practice area |
| Read before acting | How to scan and mark the approved pancake recipe; only the measurements needed | Read the same group recipe used for production, mark it, and stage a no-cook mise en place |
| Production | Just-in-time equipment and heat direction; bubbles, set edges, color, and holding | Prepare mise en place, receive authorization, cook, record actual yield, present, and reset |

## Must Happen - Core Learning

- Students locate the hand sink, dish area, instructor-confirmed emergency/exit information, and required work areas.
- Students practice Kitchen Mode, live movement callouts, handwashing, and a basic reset.
- Each group reads and marks its pancake recipe before equipment or heat use.
- Students prepare mise en place and receive instructor authorization before heat.
- The production sequence includes measurement, heat/doneness cues, teamwork, actual yield, and a complete room reset.
- No day or lab ends until storage/discard direction, dishes, sanitation, tool return, towels, trash, floors, equipment shutdown, and reset are addressed.

## If Time Remains - Flexible Learning

- Continue the orientation challenge or have groups teach one essential location.
- Repeat a kitchen route with callouts at normal working speed.
- Practice the exact measurement students need for the recipe.
- Move into the next sequence element once Core Learning is secure.
- During production, repeat a batch, compare heat adjustments, refine presentation, or extend the oral debrief.

## If Time Is Lost

- Protect essential locations, Kitchen Mode, recipe reading, the first three actions, heat authorization, and reset.
- Continue unfinished exploration or measurement practice in the next section meeting.
- Use pre-portioned ingredients, fewer pancakes, or instructor-managed holding when needed.
- If production cannot finish safely, stop at a completed batch and protect shutdown and reset.

## Clean Stopping Points

- After the essential-location debrief.
- After a complete entry/movement/reset rehearsal.
- Once each group can name ingredients, equipment, first three actions, and measuring tools.
- During production, only after a completed batch with food and equipment placed in a safe state.
- Reset is never optional at a stopping point.

## Materials and Adult Roles

- Use one [Pancake Recipe - Week 1 Group Copy](Student_Materials/Day_03_Pancake_Recipe_Reading_and_Preparation.md) per group for preparation and production.
- The Kitchen Orientation Challenge and Kitchen Mode Quick Practice are optional observation, reteaching, late-enrollment, or substitute supports.
- The Pancake Lab Checklist is a reusable group reference; oral reflection is the default unless written evidence is needed.
- **Instructor:** controls access, safety, equipment and heat authorization, demonstrations, food-distribution decisions, and final reset.
- **TA:** learns names, monitors assigned zones, prompts recipe reading and routines, supports dish/reset flow, and reports evidence; the TA does not independently authorize equipment.
