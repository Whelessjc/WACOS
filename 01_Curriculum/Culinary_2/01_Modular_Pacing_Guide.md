# Culinary 2 Modular Pacing Guide

**Current Fall 2026 Week 11 implementation:** retain the confirmed Monday Buffalo wings/chicken dip, Tuesday smoked chicken legs and Wednesday chicken Alfredo sequence from the latest teaching guide. Tuesday continuation requires the named authorized adult handoff already specified there. This full-year map supplies future-cohort opportunities and prospective priorities; it does not replace that approved near-term plan.

**Governing sequence: October 17, 2026 redesign.** Full-year depth and operations; fundamentals under pressure, with regional identity, useful variety and food students enjoy.

This is a 36-week learning spine, not a promise to cook a different dish every week. Suggested dishes are choices, not assigned recipes, new Bistro menu commitments or a requirement that every student cook all 25. For Fall 2026, apply prospectively from Week 11; completed weeks remain actual teaching history. Future cohorts use the redesigned opening.

## What stays fixed

Teach the complete work cycle: **Get ready → Read and plan → Set up → Produce → Maintain → Store and reset → Review.** Recipe-specific measuring, reading, tools, equipment authorization, safety, dish flow and reset precede first use. Later weekly labels deepen skills already introduced. Teach a sauce when a useful dish needs it; no preliminary mother-sauce survey is required by this design.

After launch, Monday–Wednesday normally protect instruction, practice and correction; Thursday is Bistro Prep and Friday is service/reset/reflection. Before launch or while service is paused, use those days for necessary instruction and rehearsal. Normally stop cooking at least 15 minutes before the bell, earlier where the task demands. Follow current operating and safety SOPs.

Weeks 1–3 form the practical opening bootcamp. Week 4 assembles and rehearses the operation without public service. Week 5 is the normal readiness-based opening target. Do not assume retained experience, independent planning or practical competence from enrollment in Culinary 2.

## Calendar and event flexibility

Use the [adopted calendar](../../00_Core/2026-07-20_Calendar_Week_Mapping.md) for dates, closures and early releases. Its older recipe suggestions do not override this pacing guide. A holiday label below is a planning window, not a confirmed school event date. Place the actual event first, then move its preparation and the displaced skill practice around it. Thanksgiving feast preparation may need to begin earlier than its window; do not force it into a shortened holiday block.

| Occasion | Useful learning | Scheduling rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Halloween | creative presentation, portioning, batch production | Choose a manageable high-interest item; joy is a legitimate goal. |
| Thanksgiving feast | vegetable/protein methods, batch yield, bread, coordinated service | Rehearse unfamiliar components before the event and spread prep across available classes. |
| Faculty holiday meal | hospitality, quantities, timing, service, full close | Build from familiar products; move adjacent instruction rather than doubling workload. |
| Valentine’s chocolates | controlled heat/process, consistent pieces, finishing | Keep the selected method and quantity within equipment, supervision and time capacity. |
| Pi Day | dough/crust, filling, moisture control, portioning | Place on the actual school observance; do not assume March 14 is a teaching day. |
| Teacher Appreciation luncheon | reliable familiar production and hospitality | Scope around testing, attendance and capstone demands; no new complex menu by default. |
| Superintendent / visitor meal | guest experience, planning, production, service | Identify what planned work it replaces before accepting a feasible scope/date. |
| Hospitality Scholars / visiting chefs | observation, useful notes, technique and career connections | Confirm the actual partner calendar; retrieve one useful note later in production. |

For each event or surprise request, decide: **What do students learn? What gets moved or removed? Can we staff, prepare, serve and close it safely within the available time?** Adjust menu, quantity, service style or date to fit. An event is applied instruction and replaces selected planned work; it does not automatically consume every protected teaching day. A fun tasting may stay a class tasting rather than becoming public production.

Maintain a visible movable slot within each planning cycle for repetition, a favorite, an event or an unexpected request. Do not fill every available block months in advance. If a deadline displaces practice, put the needed practice back on the short-term plan; remove a lower-priority application if capacity does not allow both. Avoid a year-end backlog of supposedly mandatory recipes.

## Increasing responsibility

| Stage | Student responsibility | Adult support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Modeled | Follow the demonstrated sequence and identify the next step. | Show a realistic timeline, first action, checkpoints, dish movement and clean stop. |
| Coached | Complete part of the plan and execute a familiar assignment. | Provide prompts, references, chunks and live checkpoints suited to the student. |
| Reviewed plan | Draft a familiar task sequence, quantities and reset plan. | Check feasibility before production and coach gaps. |
| Demonstrated ownership | Execute, communicate changes, maintain the station and finish. | Observe and verify; retain all safety, equipment and food-release authority. |

Movement depends on evidence, not week number. Current individual accommodations and access supports remain in force; speed or reduced prompting alone is not the standard. Recipe references, visual sequences, timers and assigned checkpoints can support competent work.

## Jobs and complete-work evidence

Instructor-built teams use applications as input alongside readiness and operational need. The five jobs are **Service, Barista, Line Cooks, Support, Expo / Floaters**. There is no mandatory station rotation. Support requires organization, communication and initiative; it is not a punishment or a default assignment for weaker students. KP identifies the adult Teaching Assistant, not a student job.

Each station maintains and cleans its own work. Support manages shared dish flow and progressive stewarding; it cannot absorb everyone’s abandoned pans, pitchers or floors. Keep Expo coverage during ticket flow; trained Floaters and Service help with assigned work when primary duties permit. Teach and inspect the middle of production, not only the final cleanup.

Use current individual assessment categories and rubrics for opening, production/service and reset/closing evidence. Existing weights remain **20% Employability / 35% Labs / 45% Summative & Bistro**. Avoid duplicate penalties or a collective failure grade. Position reassignment or a pause for an individual class’s public service may follow demonstrated inability to run the operation; communicate expectations explicitly, coach the missing work, and use supervised practice. Reopening depends on demonstrated readiness. No automatic course removal, new discipline threshold or disability-based placement rule is created.

## Week 16 — Chopped Mystery Basket Competition (All Classes)

**Scheduled capstone application:** Monday–Wednesday of instructional Week 16, subject to the adopted school calendar and instructor safety/readiness decisions. The same three-round competition is conducted in Culinary 1 and Culinary 2; performance expectations differ by course level.

- **Monday:** Round 1 — appetizer.
- **Tuesday:** Round 2 — entrée.
- **Wednesday:** Round 3 — dessert.
- **Thursday–Friday:** Preserve normal Bistro Prep and Service when operating and safe.

Teams receive a mystery basket of approximately **4–5 ingredients** each round. House rules: use every basket ingredient (not necessarily its entire quantity); pantry additions allowed; manipulate ingredients where useful; produce **two identical plated servings**; **five minutes to plan and 30 minutes to cook**; stop cooking when time expires; follow all safety, sanitation and professional work expectations; clean during judging; results are announced only after the kitchen is clean. A team chopped after a round is **eliminated from all remaining cooking rounds** and becomes part of the cleaning crew for the remainder of the three-day event, under normal safety and equipment-authorization rules. Progressive elimination intentionally reduces food production and dish volume on days two and three. Active cooking teams remain responsible for cleaning as they go and their own station resets; eliminated teams take primary responsibility for shared dish flow and assigned cleaning. Announce results only after the kitchen is clean.

**Instructional purpose:** A culminating application of familiar capabilities, not a new technique unit. Observe recipe-free planning, knife and heat control, flavor decisions, time management, communication, plating, food safety, sanitation and complete closing. Culinary 1 demonstrates usable foundations; Culinary 2 demonstrates stronger independence, judgment and consistency. Do not infer mastery from competition placement alone.

**Scheduling rule:** This three-day event replaces the ordinary Monday–Wednesday Week 16 technical sequence; displaced technique applications may be integrated into earlier/later authentic work rather than automatically crammed into Thursday/Friday. Week 16 is a preferred recurring placement, not permission to bypass required safety or student readiness.

**Judging-sheet update needed:** The existing judge's calculation sheet ranks every team across all three rounds and does not reflect progressive elimination. Adapt scoring to the teams still competing each day; confirm the ranking/tie-break procedure before publishing a revised judging sheet. Existing house-rules and calculation-sheet Word documents remain source references until updated.
