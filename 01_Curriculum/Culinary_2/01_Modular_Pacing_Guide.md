# Culinary 2 Modular Pacing Guide

This is a full-year guide for a 180-day course. It is modular on purpose.

The calendar will move. The process should not.

For 2026-2027, use `../../00_Core/2026-07-20_Calendar_Week_Mapping.md` to translate week labels into dates. The 36-week curriculum spine ends May 14, leaving May 17-27 as a testing, make-up, portfolio, inventory, and shutdown buffer.

The design is technique-first, fundamentals-under-pressure, and place-embedded.

## Opening Rule — Recalibrate to Current WACOS

Every Culinary 2 cohort begins by recalibrating to the **current** West Ashley Culinary operating system regardless of where or when prior Culinary experience occurred. Prior Culinary 1 credit is not proof of retained or current competence. The instructor may accelerate when students demonstrate the current standard.

The opening must establish Work Ready/Kitchen Ready habits, recipe/production preflight, mise en place, clean stop/handoff, food/workplace safety, communication, current Bistro teams, and the current Prep/Service systems before public service.

## Bistro Is the Performance Environment

Bistro is not a separate unit added after the fundamentals. It is where the fundamentals are organized and tested authentically.

Once Bistro is operating:

- **Monday-Wednesday — BUILD THE FUNDAMENTALS**
- **Thursday — PLAN THE WORK** using the Bistro Prep Rhythm
- **Friday — WORK THE PLAN** using the Bistro Rhythm

See `../../02_Bistro/Operations/Bistro_Rhythms.md`.

## Time Structure

| Day | Planning Note |
| --- | --- |
| Monday | New skill, correction, production, or targeted retraining from authentic evidence |
| Tuesday | Short lab, line checks, planning, targeted systems work, or approved partner programming |
| Wednesday | Longer production, practical checks, or pre-Bistro rehearsal when needed |
| Thursday | Bistro Prep after launch: Plan the Work |
| Friday | Bistro Service after launch: Work the Plan |

Build each week around realistic production time. Normally stop cooking at least 15 minutes before the bell and protect safe storage/handoff/reset.

## Module 1: Current-System Recalibration

Approximate timing: Weeks 1-3.

Do not spend the opening merely repeating Culinary 1 lessons. Use authentic work to determine what students can currently demonstrate and rebuild what is missing.

Protect:

- Work Ready: on time, Clock In, Lock In, Park It, Notebook, Pen, a Plan;
- Kitchen Ready and current safety/access requirements;
- food-safety and workplace-safety performance;
- recipe reading and Production Preflight habits;
- equipment retrieval/return;
- mise en place and workflow;
- communication and callbacks;
- clean-as-you-go;
- clean stop, storage, handoff notes, and reset;
- knife/heat/seasoning fundamentals as production vehicles;
- situational awareness: **What do you notice? What does it mean? What needs to happen next?**

Move from instructor-directed to coached to increasingly student-directed work. Culinary 2's higher standard is stronger integration and independence, not harder recipes for their own sake.

## Module 2: Assemble Bistro Systems

Approximate timing: Week 4.

Week 4 is governed by required outcomes rather than a rigid daily order. Students must experience:

- Life of an Order and the five teams: Service, Barista, Line Cooks, Support, Expo / Floaters;
- graceful-urgency Friday arrival/opening rehearsal;
- whole-group Service simulation in a populated dining room;
- Barista operating-system practice;
- situational awareness across all teams;
- Practice Bistro Prep using low-stakes/non-menu assignments;
- Production Preflight;
- clean-stop and handoff-note practice;
- whole-operation Reset & Closing rehearsal;
- cumulative safety application;
- at least one complete mock-service cycle, which may be brief and use simulated/simplified food.

Initial Service training is whole-group and instructor-led. The Teaching Assistant reinforces/coaches the established system afterward.

## Module 3: Bistro Launch + Fundamentals Under Pressure

Week 5 is the normal first-public-service target when readiness, safety, and calendar allow. The instructor may delay, reduce, modify, or pause service.

The five teams remain in each position for two consecutive services. Do not create a separate special first-service/second-service curriculum beyond the existing rotation.

Use authentic evidence to decide what needs targeted correction. Do not automatically continue planned new technical content when a fundamental operating deficiency requires immediate retraining.

Bistro Prep and Service are cumulative food-safety/workplace-safety performance environments. Culinary 2 moves toward **safety fluency under pressure**.

## Thursday — Bistro Prep Rhythm

Thursday is production work rather than a normal team lab.

- Short Bistro Huddle for current-service differences.
- Individual production assignments by default; pairs when justified.
- Monday-Thursday cleaning jobs remain active.
- Read the complete recipe/direction.
- Set station and complete mise en place.
- Production Preflight before production: product, yield, assigned quantity, scaling status, units, procedure, mise, first step.
- **No checkoff → no production.**
- Produce and clean as you go.
- Clean stop at the appropriate point.
- Store/label under current procedures, leave useful handoff notes when work continues, and reset.

## Friday — Bistro Rhythm

1. Arrive On Time and Work Ready.
2. Open Your Station.
3. Own Your Area.
4. Communicate.
5. Stay Aware — See the Whole Operation.
6. Be Useful.
7. Do the Work.
8. Clean as You Go.
9. Reset Your Station.
10. Close When Required.

Instructor teaching language may use: **I don't need to know if you can cook. I need to know if you can work.**

## Technique Modules After Launch

The existing technique progression remains valuable, but its exact week may move when authentic Bistro evidence requires targeted retraining. Drop novelty before fundamentals and preserve Thursday Prep/Friday Service when active and safe.

### Proteins, Heat, and Doneness

Focus on safe handling, thermometer use, carryover, browning, doneness judgment, resting, and simple sauces through appropriate chicken, shrimp, beef/fish, and current Bistro applications.

### Sauces, Moist Heat, Rice Dishes, and Structure

Focus on roux, reduction, emulsions, braising, simmer control, stock/soup structure, rice dishes, texture, and seasoning correction.

### Eggs, Batter, Frying, and Baking Control

Focus on egg timing, batter consistency, breading/frying systems, oil control, draining/seasoning, oven timing, and clean reset after high-mess work.

### Baking and Pastry Foundations

Focus on weighing in grams, mixing methods, doughs, custards, fermentation where approved, and temperature/timing discipline.

## Service Repetition and Application

Across the year, students increasingly:

- carry production assignments;
- run timing;
- explain techniques;
- support teammates;
- maintain standards;
- solve problems calmly;
- see the whole operation;
- complete safe handoffs and resets.

Teacher role shifts from constant directing toward observation, correction, coaching, and pressure testing while retaining final authority over safety, equipment, allergens, food release, grades, and program decisions.

## Flex Rules

When a week gets interrupted:

- Protect fundamentals and current safety needs.
- Protect Thursday Prep and Friday Service when Bistro is active and ready.
- Use authentic Bistro evidence to target Monday-Wednesday correction.
- Move the most important technical rep rather than layering it onto unstable operations.
- Drop novelty before dropping fundamentals.
- Keep Work Ready, Production Preflight, clean stop/handoff, and reset routines stable.
