# Annual Adjustment Process

This curriculum is meant to be maintained year to year.

Do not rebuild it from scratch unless the course identity changes.

## Before the Year Starts

Update:

- school calendar
- testing windows
- early release dates
- known field trips
- guest speakers
- pep rallies or major school events
- Hospitality Scholars Tuesdays
- Bistro start date
- CKC target window

Then choose:

- anchor recipes for each module
- Bistro opening menu
- likely Semester 2 baking products
- actual visiting-chef roster, feasible five-course menu and guest date

## Quarter Review Routine

At the end of each quarter, answer:

- Which routines held?
- Which routines drifted?
- Which skill needed more reps?
- Which recipe helped?
- Which recipe wasted time?
- What did Bistro expose?
- What should move earlier next year?
- What can be cut?

Keep notes short.

## What to Protect Every Year

Protect:

- stable beginning routine
- stable ending routine
- Thursday Bistro prep
- Friday Bistro service
- knife control
- recipe reading
- clean-as-you-go
- tasting and seasoning
- communication
- station reset
- composure under pressure

## What Can Change

Change:

- recipes
- Bistro menu
- capstone menu within the confirmed format
- exact week placement
- number of reps per skill
- assessment check dates
- Semester 2 module order

## CKC Review

After the CKC window, note:

- Was the confirmed credential window realistic?
- Which CKC skills were strong?
- Which skills needed more kitchen evidence?
- Did students understand the credential as part of the kitchen work?
- What line checks should be added earlier?

## Bistro Review

After each month of Bistro, note:

- strongest station
- weakest station
- recurring prep issue
- recurring communication issue
- product quality issue
- sanitation issue
- one change for next month

## Adopted opening and annual flexibility

The [October 17, 2026 redesign](../../00_Core/2026-10-17_Culinary_2_Bistro_Fundamentals_Redesign.md) adopts a three-week practical opening bootcamp for future cohorts and applies prospectively to the current year. Earlier proposals to wait until spring 2027 before adopting an opening are superseded. Use live evidence to pace the modeled → coached → reviewed-plan → ownership progression, with current individual supports.

Before the year, place confirmed seasonal/community commitments and partner dates, then choose recurring regional foods, favorites and international technique applications. Leave movable capacity for repetitions and visitor requests. Events replace planned work; recipes are not a backlog to cram in later. The confirmed capstone format remains a five-course Explore Charleston tasting menu; changing that format requires an instructor decision rather than an annual default menu swap.

## End-of-Year Reset

At the end of the year:

1. Save the working version.
2. Mark recipes as keep, revise, or replace.
3. Update line check cards.
4. Update Bistro systems.
5. Update Semester 1 pacing notes.
6. Review opening-bootcamp evidence and adjust the next cohort’s applications.
7. Leave clear notes for next year's start.

Do not over-polish the documents. They should stay usable in a real culinary lab.
