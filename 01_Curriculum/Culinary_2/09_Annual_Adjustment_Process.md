# Annual Adjustment Process

This curriculum is meant to be maintained year to year.

Do not rebuild it from scratch unless the course identity changes.

## Before the Year Starts

Update:

- school calendar
- testing windows
- early release dates
- known field trips
- guest speakers
- pep rallies or major school events
- Hospitality Scholars Tuesdays
- Bistro start date
- CKC target window

Then choose:

- anchor recipes for each module
- Bistro opening menu
- likely Semester 2 baking products
- capstone format options

## Quarter Review Routine

At the end of each quarter, answer:

- Which routines held?
- Which routines drifted?
- Which skill needed more reps?
- Which recipe helped?
- Which recipe wasted time?
- What did Bistro expose?
- What should move earlier next year?
- What can be cut?

Keep notes short.

## What to Protect Every Year

Protect:

- stable beginning routine
- stable ending routine
- Thursday Bistro prep
- Friday Bistro service
- knife control
- recipe reading
- clean-as-you-go
- tasting and seasoning
- communication
- station reset
- composure under pressure

## What Can Change

Change:

- recipes
- Bistro menu
- capstone format
- exact week placement
- number of reps per skill
- assessment check dates
- Semester 2 module order

## CKC Review

After the CKC window, note:

- Was Week 9 realistic?
- Which CKC skills were strong?
- Which skills needed more kitchen evidence?
- Did students understand the credential as part of the kitchen work?
- What line checks should be added earlier?

## Bistro Review

After each month of Bistro, note:

- strongest station
- weakest station
- recurring prep issue
- recurring communication issue
- product quality issue
- sanitation issue
- one change for next month

## 2027-2028 Opening Development

The 2027-2028 Culinary 2 opening is a proposed annual redesign, not current policy. The August 24-28, 2026 evidence shows that students may enter with highly uneven retained experience and may know safety concepts without consistently demonstrating them.

Use this working distinction:

- **Culinary 1: learn the systems.**
- **Culinary 2: integrate the systems and make decisions.**

Recipes are production vehicles in which students integrate fundamentals under pressure. Culinary 2 is not made more rigorous merely by using harder recipes.

During the 2026-2027 reset, collect concise evidence about which fundamentals students retain, which require demonstration or repetition, how quickly students regain independence, and which drills or production tasks transfer into later work. The proposed re-entry/reset should assess and develop:

- Kitchen Mode and a purposeful professional mindset
- retained Culinary 1 fundamentals
- food-safety fluency
- workplace-safety fluency
- recipe reading before acting
- complete mise en place
- prep lists
- workflow and sequencing
- backward planning from flames-off, service, bell, and reset
- communication
- teamwork
- situational awareness
- identifying bottlenecks
- remaining useful when an immediate task ends
- clean-as-you-go
- complete storage, sanitation, cleaning, and reset
- increasing independence

Use the working performance destination:

**SAFE FOOD.  
SAFE PEOPLE.  
QUALITY PRODUCT.  
PROFESSIONAL OPERATION.  
COMPLETE RESET.**

Plan a progression from **instructor-directed -> coached -> increasingly student-directed** work. Instructor authority over safety, equipment, allergens, food release, and other controlled decisions remains unchanged.

Abstract sensory or mindfulness instruction did not transfer well with the current cohort. The orange activity is not prohibited; its August 2026 implementation is evidence that transfer cannot be assumed. Train situational awareness first in concrete production using:

- **What do you notice?**
- **What does it mean?**
- **What needs to happen next?**

Review the evidence in spring 2027 before adopting a two- to four-week common-baseline opening. If approved, build a dated 2027-2028 opening package and revise annual pacing. Preserve the Culinary 2 identity and broader framework unless a separate program decision changes them. WACOS remains the continuing source of truth; each school year receives dated implementation materials rather than a separate repository.

## End-of-Year Reset

At the end of the year:

1. Save the working version.
2. Mark recipes as keep, revise, or replace.
3. Update line check cards.
4. Update Bistro systems.
5. Update Semester 1 pacing notes.
6. Review the 2027-2028 common-baseline evidence and proposed opening decision.
7. Leave clear notes for next year's start.

Do not over-polish the documents. They should stay usable in a real culinary lab.
