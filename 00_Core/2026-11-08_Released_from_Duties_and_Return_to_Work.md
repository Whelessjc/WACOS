## Scope and Grounds

Productive contribution and follow-through apply from the beginning of every class. Released from Duties applies after practical participation begins, across daily practical work, labs, prep, service, and reset. Reasons include unauthorized device use, leaving assigned work or the area without release, refusal of directions, incomplete assigned cleanup/reset, abandoned responsibilities, unsafe conduct, and repeated failure to begin or continue assigned work after clear direction and an opportunity for help.

Distinguish a need for instruction, comprehension support, or practice from refusal or repeated noncontribution. Provide coaching and approved supports. State observable behavior rather than labeling a student. Instructor judgment governs the response; the form creates no automatic discipline or grading penalty.

## Removal and Return

1. Instructor ends practical participation and states the specific reason.
2. Student completes the standing Required Learning assignment in the [reflection source](../03_Assessment/Tools/Released_from_Duties_Reflection_and_Request_to_Return.md) using existing recipes, prep lists, station checklists, or SOPs. Answer the five prompts on notebook paper; no custom adult-prepared packet or daily task selection is required. Removal does not automatically lead to another station or an easier assignment; reassignment is not the remedy for continuing noncontribution.
3. Student completes the [reflection and request](../03_Assessment/Tools/Released_from_Duties_Reflection_and_Request_to_Return.md), identifies the effect of the conduct, and proposes specific corrective actions and a readiness demonstration.
4. Student requests return. The instructor verifies demonstrated readiness; a written promise alone is insufficient. A safety-related release requires demonstration of the relevant procedure before return to that task. This does not grant new equipment authorization.
5. Instructor records Approved, Approved with conditions, or Not yet approved, with demonstrated readiness or required conditions/next steps. Conditions that must precede return must be met before participation. Instructor determines the assignment according to demonstrated readiness and operational need; the student is not guaranteed another position.

Return may occur the same day after completion of the reflection and required learning, a student request, demonstrated readiness, and instructor approval. There is no fixed minimum time out. The instructor reviews when instruction/service permits; the student continues existing assigned coursework while waiting. The Missed Lab / Make-Up Google Form remains required when applicable and does not replace reinstatement work.

Form completion and arrival on the following day do not automatically restore practical participation. Release continues until the return process is completed and the instructor approves return. Continue assigned alternate learning, approved supports, and school procedures; this is not permanent exclusion.

## Entry Readiness and Assessment

Not Kitchen Ready is a separate entry gate. A student must actually meet current entry requirements before practical participation; the next day alone does not resolve an unmet requirement. Return from Released from Duties also requires Kitchen Readiness.

Employability evidence, discipline, parent contact, and missed-lab documentation remain separate instructor decisions. Preserve current grade categories and evidence rules; do not double-penalize automatically. Alternative work may show knowledge, planning, and Professional Thinking; unperformed physical skill remains not observed until demonstrated authentically. Apply approved accommodations and school procedures. Keep completed student records only in approved school systems.

## Form Requirements

Use the current term Released from Duties and the subtitle Reflection and Request to Return. Preserve four brief prompts: event, professional expectation, effect on others/operation, and specific corrective actions with readiness demonstration. Include reasons, brief instructor notes, student request/signature, and the instructor decision/readiness field. Keep the reflection on one readable editable Word page with useful writing space; put the standing Required Learning and Request to Return directions on page two of the same document. Students write the learning responses on notebook paper. Require specific, accurate, complete explanations in their own words, using existing materials and approved learning supports. Do not impose a seat-time or word-count quota. Do not add automatic grade deductions or lengthy adult tracking.
