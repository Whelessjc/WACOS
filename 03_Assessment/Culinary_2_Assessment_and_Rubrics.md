# Assessment and Rubrics

Assessment in Culinary 2 should support practical kitchen performance.

It should not become paperwork-heavy.

## Assessment Philosophy

Students are evaluated mostly through:

- execution
- timing
- cleanliness
- communication
- organization
- follow-through
- composure under pressure

Short written checks are fine when they connect directly to kitchen application.

The kitchen is the main evidence.

## Evidence Sources

Use a mix of:

- teacher observation
- station checks
- practical performance tasks
- Bistro service evidence
- repeated patterns across line checks, used with other evidence rather than isolated-response grades
- marked recipes
- short scaling tasks
- brief reflections after service or practicals

## Current Working Scores

Use the [Assessment System](06_Assessment_System.md) for current records. Monday–Thursday labs, including Bistro Prep, use one overall 0–5 score per student per day. Do not revive the former four-level or separately scored daily lab categories.

Friday Bistro uses one overall 0–5 performance score, including reset, and separate self-reflection out of 10. Use the [Bistro rubric and calculator](Tools/Bistro_Service_Performance_Rubric.md) for adopted levels, conversion, and individual-service calculation. The Bistro mapping does not establish a lab conversion.

## Bistro Observation

Observe readiness, communication, timing, product quality, recovery, and complete reset within the single performance score. A meaningful contribution, response to coaching, and participation in reset can meet the minimum with substantial support. Repeated disengagement and significant responsibilities left for others after directions, support, and an opportunity to improve are below that minimum. Needing help alone is not failure.

## Practical Check Options

Use practical checks as short performance snapshots.

Examples:

- knife cut check with station reset
- sauteed vegetable check
- rice or grain timing check
- protein doneness check
- sauce texture and seasoning check
- soup structure check
- Bistro station readiness check
- recipe scaling and mise list check

## Grading Guidance

Keep grades tied to performance, not compliance theater.

Good gradebook categories:

- labs and production
- Bistro service
- practical checks
- short applied skills checks
- professionalism and station habits

Routine line checks reinforce learning and reveal patterns that need reteaching, demonstration, or repetition. Do not create a separate line-check grading system or turn every isolated oral response into a grade. When a direct performance check is intentionally assessed, identify it as an applied skills check and use the stated practical criteria.

Avoid grades based mostly on:

- copied notes
- long packets
- decorative projects
- generic employability worksheets

## Feedback Language

Use direct, specific feedback.

Examples:

- Your station was clean, but your timing collapsed because you did not read the full method.
- Your chicken was safe and rested correctly. Next target is stronger browning.
- You communicated the delay early. That helped the station recover.
- You finished the food but left the next crew a dirty station. That is incomplete work.
- You rushed the knife work. Slow down until the cuts are controlled.

## Reflection

Reflection should be brief.

Useful prompts:

- What worked?
- What broke down?
- What did you adjust?
- What would you do differently next time?
- What does your station need from you next service?
