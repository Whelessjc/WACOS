# Culinary 1 Line Check Cards

Line checks are short oral recall, demonstration, and scenario prompts used to reinforce Culinary 1 fundamentals.

They are not written quizzes and do not replace practical sign-offs. Use them while students are entering the kitchen, setting up, waiting for a demonstration, transitioning, or resetting.

Good line checks build:

- vocabulary and recall
- safe sequencing
- equipment awareness
- sanitation habits
- kitchen communication
- Bistro readiness
- calm decision-making

## How to Use

Use 3-5 prompts at a time, at least twice weekly. Use natural transitions: entry, setup, waiting for a demonstration or teacher check, movement between tasks, flames off, and reset. Ask one student, then another. Keep answers brief, correct misconceptions immediately, and move on.

Mix one familiar prompt with one current-unit prompt and one scenario. A missed response is a signal for a brief explanation, demonstration, or immediate repetition. Revisit missed prompts later in the class or week and track repeated individual, group, or class patterns rather than turning every isolated response into a grade.

For prompts marked **Show**, the student must demonstrate the action or point out the correct setup. A spoken answer alone is not evidence of practical competence.

If a prompt exposes a class-wide gap, reteach and practice the skill in the lab. Line checks remain an instructional reinforcement tool, not a separate assessment system. Record formal practical competence on the appropriate skills checklist or Basic Tasks Sign-Off Card.

For printable instructor materials, show the actual skill or question wording prominently; keep the card code small and secondary for reference. Use a large readable type size, reduce decorative whitespace and oversized margins, and fit the prompt plus the essential listen/look-for language efficiently on each card. Print, laminate, and cut frequently used sets for repeated kitchen use.

## Personal Readiness and Daily Systems

| Card | Prompt | Listen/Look For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PR1 | What should you have with you when class begins? | correct uniform, closed-toe shoes, hair restraint, notebook, pen |
| PR2 | What should happen before you touch food or equipment? | enter calmly, read the agenda, store belongings, wash hands, set the station |
| PR3 | When must you wash your hands? | before food work; after contamination, restroom use, face/hair/phone contact, raw food, trash, or cleaning |
| PR4 | Do gloves replace handwashing? | no; wash first and change gloves between tasks or after contamination |
| PR5 | What does "flames off" mean? | stop cooking early enough to complete cleanup and teacher check |
| PR6 | When are you finished with a lab? | product handled, station reset, tools returned, waste handled, teacher check complete |
| PR7 | **Show:** How do you report a spill or unsafe condition? | stop or warn others, communicate clearly, protect the area, get help, correct it safely |
| PR8 | What should you do after making a mistake? | own it promptly, communicate, protect safety/product, ask for the correct recovery step |

## Hand Sink, Warewashing, and Sanitizer

| Card | Prompt | Listen/Look For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WS1 | What belongs at a stocked handwashing sink? | warm running water, soap, paper towels, trash can, handwashing sign |
| WS2 | What is the correct three-compartment sink order? | wash, rinse, sanitize, air-dry |
| WS3 | Why must dishes air-dry? | towels can recontaminate surfaces; sanitizer needs contact time |
| WS4 | What is the difference between cleaning and sanitizing? | cleaning removes food/soil; sanitizing reduces pathogens to a safe level |
| WS5 | How do you know sanitizer is correctly mixed? | use the correct test strip and compare it with the product's required range |
| WS6 | What should you do if sanitizer tests outside the required range? | stop, remake or adjust it, and test again; do not guess |
| WS7 | Where should wiping towels stay between uses? | in correctly mixed sanitizer, unless the current procedure requires otherwise |
| WS8 | What must be scraped or removed before warewashing? | food and debris |
| WS9 | What should be checked before using the dish machine? | machine is assembled and ready, chemicals supplied, racks clear, temperatures/indicators correct |
| WS10 | What happens at dish-station shutdown? | drain/break down as directed, clean traps/screens and surfaces, return items, leave area reset |

## Food Safety and Temperature Control

| Card | Prompt | Listen/Look For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FS1 | What is cross-contamination? | harmful transfer between food, hands, tools, or surfaces |
| FS2 | How do you prevent raw food from contaminating ready-to-eat food? | separate storage and work areas, correct boards/tools, handwashing, cleaning and sanitizing |
| FS3 | Where should a thermometer probe go? | thickest part or thermal center, away from bone and pan surfaces |
| FS4 | What must happen to a thermometer probe before and after use? | clean and sanitize it |
| FS5 | **Show:** How do you calibrate a thermometer using ice water? | make a proper ice-water slurry, place probe correctly without touching container, wait, adjust/record per thermometer procedure |
| FS6 | What should you do if you do not know a required cooking or holding temperature? | stop and verify it from the approved reference or instructor; never guess |
| FS7 | Name safe ways to thaw food. | under refrigeration; under controlled running water; during cooking when approved; microwave only when cooking immediately |
| FS8 | Where should food never be thawed? | at room temperature or in standing warm water |
| FS9 | How do shallow pans and an ice bath help cooling? | increase surface area and remove heat faster |
| FS10 | When cooling food, what should be monitored? | time and temperature; stir when appropriate and follow the approved cooling limits |
| FS11 | What should happen if food has been in an unsafe condition and you are unsure it is safe? | stop, isolate it, tell the instructor, and do not serve it |
| FS12 | What is an allergen cross-contact risk? | allergen transferred through hands, tools, surfaces, oil, or ingredients to another order |

## Labels, Storage, and Receiving Awareness

| Card | Prompt | Listen/Look For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LS1 | What information belongs on a kitchen label? | product name, required date information, class/group or initials, and allergen information when required |
| LS2 | What is the difference between a prep date and a discard/use-by date? | when it was prepared versus the last approved day for use |
| LS3 | What does FIFO mean? | first in, first out; use older approved product before newer product |
| LS4 | What should happen to an old label when a container is washed or reused? | remove it completely; never layer a new label over unclear old information |
| LS5 | How should ready-to-eat food and raw proteins be separated in storage? | ready-to-eat above raw; raw items ordered to prevent drips/cross-contamination |
| LS6 | Where may chemicals be stored? | in their designated area, separated from food and food-contact items |
| LS7 | Why must food and supplies stay off the floor? | prevents contamination and supports cleaning and inspection |
| LS8 | What should you check when receiving or putting away a product? | temperature when required, package condition, dates, labels, quality, and correct storage location |
| LS9 | What should you do with damaged, leaking, swollen, or questionable packaging? | isolate it and ask the instructor; do not accept or use it automatically |
| LS10 | **Show:** Place this item in the correct storage location. | correct area, separation, FIFO position, label visible, container covered |

## Recipe Reading, Measuring, and Kitchen Math

| Card | Prompt | Listen/Look For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| RM1 | What should you read before touching ingredients? | the entire recipe, including yield, ingredients, method, times, temperatures, and notes |
| RM2 | What does yield tell you? | how much the recipe produces or how many portions it serves |
| RM3 | What should you mark on a recipe before cooking? | times, temperatures, equipment, divided ingredients, hazards, and quality cues |
| RM4 | What is mise en place? | physical and mental readiness: plan, ingredients, tools, sequence, and clean station |
| RM5 | Why does ingredient order matter? | it follows the method and can change texture, safety, or final quality |
| RM6 | What is the difference between a dry measuring cup and a liquid measuring cup? | dry measure is leveled; liquid measure is read at eye level on a flat surface |
| RM7 | How do you measure a small dry ingredient with a spoon? | fill the correct spoon and level it unless the recipe says otherwise |
| RM8 | What is the first step in scaling a recipe? | identify original yield and desired yield |
| RM9 | How do you find a recipe's scaling factor? | desired yield divided by original yield |
| RM10 | What should you do if recipe directions and a demonstration seem different? | stop and ask before guessing |

## Scenario Cards

| Card | Scenario | Expected Move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SC1 | Your sanitizer test strip shows the solution is outside the required range. | Stop using it, remake or adjust it according to procedure, and retest. |
| SC2 | You find an unlabeled container in the cooler. | Do not guess; isolate it and ask the instructor for disposition. |
| SC3 | Raw chicken liquid drips onto a ready-to-eat item. | Stop, report it, discard/isolate as directed, clean and sanitize, then correct storage. |
| SC4 | A classmate drops a knife. | Warn others, step back, let it fall, then retrieve it safely after it stops. |
| SC5 | Your recipe makes 4 portions, but your group needs 12. | Calculate a factor of 3, scale every ingredient, and check the math before cooking. |
| SC6 | You touch your phone while wearing gloves. | Remove gloves, wash hands, and put on new gloves if the next task requires them. |
| SC7 | A guest mentions a food allergy. | Stop, notify the instructor/manager, verify the order and ingredients, and prevent cross-contact; never promise without checking. |
| SC8 | You do not know whether a food has cooled safely. | Do not serve or store it as safe; isolate it and ask the instructor to review time/temperature evidence. |
| SC9 | A machine cord is frayed. | Do not plug it in; remove it from use and report it immediately. |
| SC10 | Your group finishes cooking while another group is still working. | Begin the full reset, check shared-area needs, and help only when assigned or invited. |
| SC11 | A ticket is late and your item is not ready. | Communicate honestly and early, give a useful time estimate, and ask for help if needed. |
| SC12 | You notice a class-wide missed skill during line checks. | Pause for a brief reteach, demonstrate, practice, and verify it during the lab. |

## Suggested Rotation by Course Phase

| Course Phase | Priority Card Groups |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks 1-2: Orientation and safety | PR, WS, FS, EK |
| Weeks 3-5: Systems before cooking | WS, FS, LS, RM |
| Weeks 4-6: Bistro launch foundations | KB, PR, LS, scenario cards |
| Weeks 6-10: Knife and basic cooking | EK, RM, CF, FS |
| Weeks 10-14: Applied labs | RM, CF, FS, scenario cards |
| After Bistro launch | KB, LS, WS, PR, scenario cards |
| Weeks 17-18: Culinary 2 bridge | Mixed cards, with missed fundamentals prioritized |

The instructor may print, laminate, and cut the table rows into reusable cards or display prompts digitally. When a Daily Teaching Guide or printable instructor sheet names selected line checks, write the actual prompt or skill wording rather than relying mainly on card codes.
