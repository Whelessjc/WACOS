# Assessment System

## Purpose

West Ashley Culinary uses concise observation records to support instructor judgment in a working high-school kitchen. The system gathers defensible evidence without requiring continuous observation or long narratives during labs and Bistro service.

The current quarter grade structure remains:

- 45% Summative Assessments: Projects, Tests, and Bistro.
- 35% Formative Assessments and Labs.
- 20% Employability Skills.

Bistro evidence stays in Summative Assessments: Projects, Tests, and Bistro. Lab evidence stays in Formative Assessments and Labs. Employability evidence stays in Employability Skills.

### Recording and Adult Coverage

1. Name the lab target or Bistro assignment and expected standard.
2. Instructor and Teaching Assistant identify observation coverage before work begins.
3. Observe natural checkpoints; record meaningful evidence and concise exceptions.
4. Record the daily lab score or the three Friday Bistro components. Keep reassignment, partial attendance, and missing-work context distinct.
5. Combine adult evidence; the instructor resolves differences and assigns final grades.

Reflection supports direct evidence; it does not replace observed physical skill.

Group success or failure does not automatically become every student's individual grade.

## Employability Assessment Procedure

Employability is 20% of the quarter grade and measures observable professional habits, not attitude or generic participation. Enter one Employability grade each week. Use the [Employability Observation Log](Tools/Employability_Observation_Log.md) as a fast paper readiness and exception record.

**Employability is professional development happening now.** Useful instructional language is **SHOW UP. BE READY. PAY ATTENTION. BE USEFUL. KEEP LEARNING.** Treat the behaviors—not the phrase as branding—as the curriculum: punctuality, work readiness, appropriate dress, pen/notebook use, presence, coachability, initiative, awareness, anticipation, helping, research, and maintaining a current resume.

Normal performance requires no daily narrative. Routine operational questions should show investigation before escalation when practical; safety, allergen, food-handling, and equipment-authorization questions should go to an adult immediately. Record concise evidence for:

- readiness, punctuality, uniform, and required materials;
- **daily workflow readiness: issued pocket notebook and pen out, with the immediate task or workflow written before work begins;**
- communication, teamwork, initiative, and coachability;
- station responsibility and productive use of time;
- cleanup, reset, and stewardship of the kitchen and equipment;
- exceptional ownership or leadership;
- coaching, repeated concerns, or significant incidents.

The pocket notebook is a working culinary tool. Students may use it for recipes, techniques, notes, ideas, workflow, prep lists, station tasks, service notes, questions, and reminders. It is not a separate daily journal or standalone graded assignment. Repeated failure to bring the required notebook/pen or to prepare the immediate workflow may become readiness evidence; one isolated omission should be handled through normal coaching unless the broader pattern warrants formal evidence.

### Evidence Cycle

1. State and teach the standards.
2. Give timely coaching when a concern appears.
3. Record a brief note when evidence is significant, repeated, exceptional, or needed to show a pattern.
4. Tell the student when a concern is becoming formal grade evidence and name the improvement needed.
5. Look for later evidence of response and improvement.
6. Before finalizing the grading-period grade, let the student review the evidence and add relevant context or identify missing evidence.
7. The instructor assigns the holistic grade from the pattern.

| Employability band | Teacher-facing evidence guide |
|---|---|
| Exceeds expectations | Consistently meets normal expectations and shows repeated, meaningful ownership, initiative, leadership, or stewardship. |
| Consistently meets expectations | The overall pattern is dependable. Isolated minor coaching is followed by correction and does not define the period. |
| Inconsistently meets expectations | A repeated pattern in one or more standards requires coaching and is not yet dependable, though improvement may be visible. |
| Does not yet meet expectations | Significant or persistent evidence shows the student is not yet meeting core professional-habit expectations. |
| Insufficient evidence | Attendance, assignment, or other circumstances leave too little evidence for a defensible decision. |

Use the baseline-four direction and pending implementation rules above. The former automatic 5/10-point deduction and 5-point bonus system is superseded. Keep feedback and exception evidence concise. Provide an evidence-review opportunity each grading period; the precise quarterly-conference schedule remains an instructor implementation decision. Safety and kitchen-access responses remain separate from grade calculation.

### Kitchen Readiness and Missed Practical Work

Daily Kitchen Readiness requires ID, proper footwear, notebook, and pen. A student who does not meet these requirements does not enter the kitchen for that day's practical work and completes the current handwritten Kitchen Alternate Learning Assignment tied to the actual Learning Target. When a lab is missed, the student also completes the online Culinary Missed Lab / Make-Up Form.

**Alternate Learning Assignment = in-period learning. Missed Lab / Make-Up Form = documentation and initiation of the existing recovery process.** Alternate work can demonstrate knowledge, planning, and Professional Thinking. Physical skill that was not performed remains `NO`/not observed unless later demonstrated through an authentic instructor-approved opportunity. The program does not routinely recreate missed labs.

Use **Released from Duties** when a student entered practical work but the instructor ends practical participation for the day because continued participation is inappropriate due to behavior during class/lab. This is distinct from **Not Kitchen Ready**, which is an entry/readiness gate. The current Released from Duties form/reflection must be completed before return to the privilege of kitchen-lab participation. Employability evidence, discipline, parent contact, missed-lab documentation, and other school responses remain separate instructor decisions; do not double-penalize automatically.

## Assessment Consistency Rules

- The instructor makes the final grading decision.
- A checklist mark is evidence, not an automatic grade by itself.
- `NO` or not observed is not equivalent to failure.
- Safety interventions are documented separately when necessary, and safety, discipline, and final grades remain under instructor authority.
- Students receive timely feedback.
- Repeated evidence is more reliable than one isolated moment.
- Student reflection may support but cannot replace direct performance evidence.
- Peer comments may be formative but do not determine another student's grade.
- Group performance does not automatically become every student's individual grade.
- Do not double-penalize the same behavior in multiple grade categories. Record it where it best matches the learning target; use another category only when separate evidence supports a distinct standard.
- Honor accommodations and approved supports without lowering unrelated learning standards.
- Assess the learning target emphasized in the lesson, lab, or service.
- When a score differs substantially from the recorded pattern, the instructor adds a brief evidence-based note.
- Student-facing versions require instructor review before use.

For an excused absence, enter 1 to signify missing work. The student has five days to complete an instructor-approved makeup; maximum scores are 90 on day 1, 80 on day 2, 70 on day 3, 60 on day 4, and 50 on day 5. After day 5, the grade becomes 0. Labs or applied skills may be reassessed case by case through a direct demonstration or focused practical. Reassessment is not automatic and does not replace safety or discipline responses.


## Bistro Job Observation and Support

Current Bistro jobs are **Service, Barista, Line Cooks, Support, and Expo / Floaters**. Students are placed rather than automatically rotated. Observation follows the student's assigned job while still measuring transferable Bistro expectations such as readiness, safety, communication, productive use of time, station ownership, situational awareness, Clean as You Go, reset, and closing. When observing Expo / Floaters, distinguish the Expo anchor from authorized floating work: one student remains accountable for Expo/pass during active service while other trained team members deploy.
