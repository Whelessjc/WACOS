# Assessment System

## Purpose

West Ashley Culinary uses concise observation records to support instructor judgment in a working high-school kitchen. The system gathers defensible evidence without requiring continuous observation or long narratives during labs and Bistro service.

The current quarter grade structure remains:

- 45% Summative Assessments: Projects, Tests, and Bistro.
- 35% Formative Assessments and Labs.
- 20% Employability Skills.

Bistro evidence stays in Summative Assessments: Projects, Tests, and Bistro. Lab evidence stays in Formative Assessments and Labs. Employability evidence stays in Employability Skills.

## Common Evidence Language

Live Bistro records use fast letter codes. Lab records use a four-level scale because five evidence areas are scored after the lab rather than continuously during service.

| Context | Evidence language |
|---|---|
| Bistro | `E` exceptional evidence or leadership; `M` meets; `C` coaching needed; `N` standard not met; `NO` not observed or insufficient evidence |
| Lab | `4` exceeds the current standard; `3` meets; `2` approaches; `1` does not yet meet; `NO` not observed or insufficient evidence |
| Employability | `+` exceptional ownership or leadership; `C` coaching; `R` repeated concern; `I` significant incident; blank means no exception recorded |

`E/M/C/N/NO` is Bistro-only observation language. Lab and Employability records retain their current context-specific scales.

Codes are evidence, not automatic grades. A serious safety, sanitation, or conduct issue requires immediate intervention and separate instructor review when needed.

## Bistro Manager Observation Method

Use the [Bistro Manager Observation Record](Tools/Bistro_Manager_Observation_Record.md) during opening/readiness, active service, and closing/reset. The instructor and Teacher Assistant may divide the room by named students, team, station, or service area. Current teams are Dining, Kitchen, Support, and Barista.

1. Before service, each observer writes initials and assigned coverage.
2. During each natural observation window, record one quick code when useful. Do not narrate routine performance.
3. Add a short note only for exceptional evidence, a significant concern, an incident, or context needed for instructor review.
4. After service, combine both records by student. Preserve differing observations; do not average them mechanically.
5. The instructor reviews the pattern, relevant notes, reassignment or attendance context, reflection, and any other direct evidence, then assigns the final grade.

The behaviors behind each window are:

- **Opening/readiness:** enters Kitchen Mode ready, understands the assignment, establishes readiness, and identifies missing mise en place, supplies, or information.
- **Active service:** performs safely and correctly, maintains organization and sanitation, communicates, responds to coaching and changes, and protects the standard appropriate to the assignment.
- **Closing/reset:** completes shutdown and cleaning, stores and returns items correctly, resets for the next group, and remains engaged until released.

### Bistro Holistic Bands

| Band | Evidence pattern |
|---|---|
| Exceeds the service standard | Meets the assigned standard across observed windows and shows meaningful exceptional evidence, leadership, recovery, or support without taking over. |
| Meets the service standard | Meets the assigned standard across the available evidence; minor coaching does not outweigh competent service. |
| Approaching the service standard | Coaching or an unmet standard is a meaningful part of the pattern, but the student shows partial success, response, or recovery. |
| Does not yet meet the service standard | Repeated or significant unmet standards show that the student did not yet perform the assignment safely, correctly, or reliably. |
| Insufficient evidence | Available evidence cannot support a defensible judgment. |

Exact numeric ranges for Canvas or gradebook entry require instructor approval before adoption. Do not invent precision by averaging codes. A recommended implementation is one approved narrow value or range per band, entered only after the instructor confirms the conversion.

### Bistro Evidence Exceptions

- **Absence:** follow the confirmed attendance and missing-work process. Do not convert absence or `NO` directly to zero.
- **Reassignment:** assess the work actually assigned and note the change.
- **Partial service:** use the windows observed and seek another relevant source when needed.
- **Insufficient evidence:** use later service, a practical check, direct questioning, reflection plus direct evidence, reassessment, or the confirmed missing-work process.
- **Observer disagreement:** retain both records. The instructor checks assignment, location, timing, notes, and other evidence and makes the final decision.

Use the [Bistro Observer Calibration Guide](Tools/Bistro_Observer_Calibration_Guide.md) before the first service and briefly when coding drifts.

## Minimalist Lab Assessment Procedure

Use the [Lab Observation Record](Tools/Lab_Observation_Record.md) for Culinary 1 and Culinary 2. The five evidence areas remain the same; the expected independence, pace, complexity, and quality change with the course and stated learning target.

1. Name the lab target and choose no more than two focus criteria.
2. Mark planned instructor checkpoints and whether a product-quality check is appropriate.
3. Observe at natural moments. Record exceptions and decisive evidence rather than every action.
4. After the lab, assign one level in each evidence area that was actually available for assessment.
5. Use the short student reflection as supporting evidence. Reflection cannot replace direct performance evidence.
6. Review safety or sanitation interventions separately before finalizing the grade.

The five evidence areas are readiness and planning; safe, sanitary, and organized work; technique and process; product result; and shutdown, reset, and reflection. Product quality should reflect the stated target and should not outweigh technique, safety, or process when those are the lesson focus.

Routine line checks are instructional reinforcement: use missed responses for brief reteaching, demonstration, immediate repetition, and pattern tracking rather than grading each isolated answer. When the instructor deliberately schedules a separate applied skills check with direct demonstration, use the same record with only the target, one or two focus criteria, checkpoint, direct demonstration, and result completed. Unused areas are `NO`, not failures.

### Partner and Group Labs

Record the shared product only once when appropriate, but determine each student's grade from individual evidence: assigned work, direct technique, explanation, checkpoint response, contribution to reset, and reflection. Group success or failure does not automatically become every student's individual grade.

### Reassessment and Insufficient Evidence

Use the current WACOS reassessment or missing-work process when confirmed for the class. Until exact implementation is confirmed, the instructor may collect another direct demonstration, a focused skills check, a later lab observation, or a conference supported by direct evidence. `NO` is not a zero.

## Employability Assessment Procedure

Employability is 20% of the quarter grade and measures observable professional habits, not attitude or generic participation. Use the [Employability Observation Log](Tools/Employability_Observation_Log.md) to record exceptions across a defined grading period.

Normal performance requires no daily narrative. Record concise evidence for:

- readiness, punctuality, uniform, and required materials;
- communication, teamwork, initiative, and coachability;
- station responsibility and productive use of time;
- cleanup, reset, and stewardship of the kitchen and equipment;
- exceptional ownership or leadership;
- coaching, repeated concerns, or significant incidents.

### Evidence Cycle

1. State and teach the standards.
2. Give timely coaching when a concern appears.
3. Record a brief note when evidence is significant, repeated, exceptional, or needed to show a pattern.
4. Tell the student when a concern is becoming formal grade evidence and name the improvement needed.
5. Look for later evidence of response and improvement.
6. Before finalizing the grading-period grade, let the student review the evidence and add relevant context or identify missing evidence.
7. The instructor assigns the holistic grade from the pattern.

| Employability band | Teacher-facing evidence guide |
|---|---|
| Exceeds expectations | Consistently meets normal expectations and shows repeated, meaningful ownership, initiative, leadership, or stewardship. |
| Consistently meets expectations | The overall pattern is dependable. Isolated minor coaching is followed by correction and does not define the period. |
| Inconsistently meets expectations | A repeated pattern in one or more standards requires coaching and is not yet dependable, though improvement may be visible. |
| Does not yet meet expectations | Significant or persistent evidence shows the student is not yet meeting core professional-habit expectations. |
| Insufficient evidence | Attendance, assignment, or other circumstances leave too little evidence for a defensible decision. |

Coaching notes are formative until the instructor identifies them as part of a repeated or significant pattern used for the grade. Significant incidents may become formal evidence immediately after instructor review. The prior weekly 100-point automatic-deduction model is retired. Exact numeric conversions and the frequency of gradebook entry remain unconfirmed and must not be invented.

## Assessment Consistency Rules

- The instructor makes the final grading decision.
- A checklist mark is evidence, not an automatic grade by itself.
- `NO` or not observed is not equivalent to failure.
- Safety interventions are documented separately when necessary, and safety, discipline, and final grades remain under instructor authority.
- Students receive timely feedback.
- Repeated evidence is more reliable than one isolated moment.
- Student reflection may support but cannot replace direct performance evidence.
- Peer comments may be formative but do not determine another student's grade.
- Group performance does not automatically become every student's individual grade.
- Do not double-penalize the same behavior in multiple grade categories. Record it where it best matches the learning target; use another category only when separate evidence supports a distinct standard.
- Honor accommodations and approved supports without lowering unrelated learning standards.
- Assess the learning target emphasized in the lesson, lab, or service.
- When a score differs substantially from the recorded pattern, the instructor adds a brief evidence-based note.
- Student-facing versions require instructor review before use.
